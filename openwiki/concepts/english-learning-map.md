# English Learning Contexts Map

Use this page as a navigation layer, not a replacement lesson. The [English: Workplace Vocabulary & Idioms topic](../../topics/english-workplace-vocabulary.md) provides the curated sequence. It assumes basic comprehension of simple conversations and orders concrete, situation-bound vocabulary before transferable verbs and workplace idioms.

## Start here: the curated progression

Follow the [workplace vocabulary topic](../../topics/english-workplace-vocabulary.md) in its published order when you want a complete path. It moves through two concrete settings before introducing idioms:

1. **Construction work:** tools, the deck being built, plans, and compliance.
2. **Supermarket checkout:** the transaction vocabulary, then the transferable verb *expire*.
3. **Workplace and everyday interaction:** guidelines, effort, leniency, quality, and minor bad luck.

Use a context entrypoint below if you instead need English for a situation you are encountering now. Rejoin the topic sequence after the focused lesson rather than treating this page as a separate curriculum.

## Construction: from object names to job expectations

Begin with [Construction Tools Vocabulary](../../concepts/english/construction-tools-vocabulary.md). It supplies the concrete tool names and the compound-noun naming pattern that anchors the construction dialogue. Its explicit related links connect that entrypoint to the rest of this job-site cluster.

| If you need to express or understand… | Go to the canonical concept | Why this is the next route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the outdoor structure being discussed | [Deck as Backyard Platform Vocabulary](../../concepts/english/deck-backyard-platform-vocabulary.md) | It identifies the construction object, separating this sense of *deck* from other meanings. |
| drawings and instructions for the project | [Construction Plans Vocabulary](../../concepts/english/construction-plans-vocabulary.md) | It distinguishes construction plans from ordinary personal intentions and connects plans to the tools and deck. |
| inspection, safety, or a required standard | [Up to Code Phrase](../../concepts/english/up-to-code-phrase.md) | It separates the external compliance requirement from merely good-looking work. |
| practical advice rather than a strict instruction | [Idiom: Rule of Thumb](../../concepts/english/rule-of-thumb-idiom.md) | It is explicitly related to the tools and plans material and frames experience-based guidance as non-absolute. |
| quality care, praise, or a gentle rebuke about sloppy work | [Idiom: Take Pride in Your Work](../../concepts/english/take-pride-in-your-work.md) | It is explicitly related to the tools and rule-of-thumb material and distinguishes self-description from criticism. |

The construction route has a useful boundary: plans describe what to build, while *up to code* names the external standard the finished work must meet. A rule of thumb may guide judgment, but it is not the formal project instruction. Route to the canonical lessons for the language details and examples.

## Supermarket checkout: transaction language to reusable language

Choose [Supermarket Checkout Vocabulary](../../concepts/english/supermarket-checkout-vocabulary.md) when the immediate situation is paying for groceries. Its published flow runs from the checkout lane and belt through bag choice, coupons, payment, and receipt. This provides a second concrete setting before the path becomes more abstract.

- Continue to [Expire as an Everyday Validity Verb](../../concepts/english/expire-everyday-validity-verb.md) when the key issue is that a coupon, card, license, document, membership, or food item is no longer valid or usable. The checkout concept explicitly introduces the word through coupons; the dedicated concept generalizes it.
- Continue to [Idiom: Cut Me Some Slack](../../concepts/english/cut-me-some-slack-idiom.md) when someone is requesting informal leniency. It is explicitly related to the checkout context and to *it’s not my day*.
- Continue to [Idiom: It’s Not My Day](../../concepts/english/its-not-my-day-idiom.md) when the intended tone is a light acknowledgement of a run of small setbacks. The concept connects it to the checkout dialogue and to *cut me some slack*.

## Idiom selection after the context vocabulary

The topic places the idioms after the concrete vocabulary. Pick the canonical lesson by communicative purpose rather than by memorizing them as interchangeable workplace phrases:

| Purpose | Canonical lesson | Distinction to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| offer a usually useful, experience-based guideline | [Idiom: Rule of Thumb](../../concepts/english/rule-of-thumb-idiom.md) | A practical principle with room for exceptions, not an exact rule. |
| motivate maximum effort under pressure | [Idiom: Give 110 Percent](../../concepts/english/give-110-percent-idiom.md) | Figurative maximum effort; high effort alone does not establish compliance. |
| ask another person to be less strict | [Idiom: Cut Me Some Slack](../../concepts/english/cut-me-some-slack-idiom.md) | An informal request for leniency. |
| emphasize care and satisfaction in the result, or criticize carelessness | [Idiom: Take Pride in Your Work](../../concepts/english/take-pride-in-your-work.md) | Quality and care, with a positive self-use and a corrective use toward someone else. |
| acknowledge minor bad luck lightly | [Idiom: It’s Not My Day](../../concepts/english/its-not-my-day-idiom.md) | A casual response to several small things going wrong. |
