# The 2026 State of AI Agents Report

## Summary

Anthropic and Material surveyed more than 500 US technical leaders in late 2025 and paired the results with customer case studies and consulting-firm perspectives. The report argues that enterprise agents are moving from isolated assistance into multi-stage and cross-functional workflows: 57% of respondents reported multi-stage use, 16% reported cross-functional or end-to-end use, and 81% planned more complex projects in 2026. Coding is the most widespread application, while data analysis, reporting, and internal process automation lead the use cases beyond software development.

The report frames adoption as a socio-technical systems problem. Integration with existing systems, implementation cost, and accessible high-quality data are major constraints; smaller organizations also report employee resistance and training needs. Its recommended direction is not simply to attach agents to unchanged processes, but to redesign workflows, decision rights, quality controls, and workforce practices around them. A hybrid build strategy is common because packaged products provide speed while custom components provide workflow-specific control.

Reported value is substantial but should be interpreted carefully: 80% of surveyed leaders said agents already produce measurable economic impact, and featured companies report large improvements in cycle time, access to expertise, and automation. These figures are self-reported and the cases are selected by an AI vendor, so they establish useful hypotheses and implementation patterns rather than independent causal proof. The strongest transferable lessons are to measure business outcomes rather than token cost, make organizational context accessible to agents, validate high-stakes systems against domain experts, preserve human oversight, and measure whether automation shifts people toward judgment, relationships, and skill development.

## Knowledge Map

- Progression from single-task assistance to multi-stage and cross-functional agent workflows
- Hybrid acquisition: packaged agents plus custom components where differentiation matters
- Context and data accessibility as prerequisites for sophisticated agent work
- Workflow, authority, and workforce redesign around agent delegation
- Domain-calibrated evaluation for regulated or high-stakes deployments
- Business-value measurement beyond model cost and raw task speed

## Key Takeaways

- Treat agent adoption as an integration, data, and change-management program—not a model purchase.
- Redesign the end-to-end workflow instead of adding an agent to every existing step.
- Start with measurable, bounded work, then increase autonomy and organizational scope as evidence accumulates.
- Reuse proven prompts, commands, and agent patterns so successful practices spread across teams.
- Evaluate consequential systems against real domain cases and expert judgments before scaling.
- Track both economic results and whether human time moves toward higher-leverage work.
- Separate the report's directional evidence from its vendor-selected examples and self-reported survey claims.
