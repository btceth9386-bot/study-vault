# Business-Outcome-Aligned AI Use-Case Selection

- **One-sentence definition**: Business-outcome-aligned AI use-case selection chooses projects by the business problem they can improve, the value that can be measured, and the practical cost and risk of delivering them.
- **Why it exists / what problem it solves**: Starting with a flashy AI feature can create a demo that nobody needs. Starting with a real pain point gives the pilot a testable reason to exist.
- **Keywords**: use case, ROI, feasibility, adoption, implementation effort
- **Related concepts**: [[agentic-engineering-tco-curve]], [[risk-bounded-ai-pilot-design]], [[agent-centered-workflow-redesign]], [[agentic-workflow-complexity-ladder]]
- **Depth**: 2/4
- **Last updated**: 2026-09-26
- **Source**: The Enterprise AI Transformation Guide

## Summary

Choose an AI project the same way you would choose any business investment: name the problem, state the expected benefit, and check whether the organization can actually deliver it. Compare potential value with technical feasibility, user willingness to change, disruption risk, and required time and money. This prevents an enterprise from trying to transform everything at once. It also gives a pilot a clear result to measure before anyone argues for a wider rollout.

## Example

Instead of selecting a general-purpose chatbot because it is available, a legal team targets slow contract review. It estimates time saved per contract, checks whether the documents can be accessed safely, tests whether reviewers will use drafts, and compares the effort with other candidate projects.
