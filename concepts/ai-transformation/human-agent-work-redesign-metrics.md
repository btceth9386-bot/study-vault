# Human-Agent Work Redesign Metrics

- **One-sentence definition**: Human-agent work redesign metrics measure whether automation moves people toward judgment, relationships, learning, and other higher-leverage work rather than only counting tasks or hours saved.
- **Why it exists / what problem it solves**: Speed and cost can hide whether employees gained useful capacity and skills or merely inherited review and exception work.
- **Keywords**: work composition, higher-leverage work, skills, strategic work, adoption metrics
- **Related concepts**: [[multidimensional-ai-pilot-scorecard]], [[role-based-ai-capability-building]]
- **Depth**: 2/4
- **Last updated**: 2026-09-26
- **Source**: The 2026 State of AI Agents Report

## Summary

Saving an hour is not automatically an improvement if that hour becomes more low-value checking and cleanup. These metrics ask what people now spend their time doing: more judgment, customer relationships, strategic work, and skill development—or more exception handling. They complement ordinary efficiency and quality metrics by showing whether the redesigned work makes the team more capable over time.

## Example

After deploying a sales-support agent, a team records not only response time but also the share of time representatives spend with customers, planning accounts, and learning. If response time improves but representatives spend the saved time correcting bad agent outputs, the redesign has not yet produced the intended human benefit.

## Relationship to existing concepts

- [[multidimensional-ai-pilot-scorecard]]: Tracks adoption, efficiency, quality, and satisfaction; these metrics add the composition and developmental value of human work.
- [[role-based-ai-capability-building]]: Helps people use their reallocated time to develop the capabilities that make it productive.
