# Multidimensional AI Pilot Scorecard

- **One-sentence definition**: A multidimensional AI pilot scorecard tracks adoption, efficiency, output quality, and user satisfaction on the same timeline.
- **Why it exists / what problem it solves**: A tool can save time but create errors, or be accurate but unused. Several measures expose those trade-offs.
- **Keywords**: adoption, efficiency, quality, satisfaction, review cadence
- **Related concepts**: [[llm-observability]], [[human-agent-work-redesign-metrics]]
- **Depth**: 2/4
- **Last updated**: 2026-09-26
- **Source**: The Enterprise AI Transformation Guide

## Summary

Do not let one flattering number decide whether an AI pilot worked. Track whether people use it, whether work is faster, whether results meet a quality bar, and whether users would keep using it. Review the signals regularly and include qualitative feedback, because friction and workarounds often explain a metric better than the metric alone.

## Example

A contract-review pilot cuts average review time from two hours to thirty minutes, but reviewers report frequent missing clauses. The scorecard shows that efficiency improved while quality needs work before rollout.

## Relationship to existing concepts

- [[llm-observability]]: Observability supplies usage, latency, cost, and quality evidence that can populate the scorecard.
- [[human-agent-work-redesign-metrics]]: Adds whether automation improves the kind of work people can do, not just task-level results.
